## Frontpage interpretation

Working direction: **emissive marks**. Let a source-backed filament, phosphor patch, glint or luminous pigment exceed the surrounding SDR plate's white on a genuinely supported HDR display. Keep the rest of the image restrained; brightness separation is the material, not a generic CSS bloom or a full-screen white field. Borrow the display behavior, not the portrait.

- Add to the optional repertoire, not to every edition. Select only when owning source/plate material makes emission meaningful; zero effects remains valid.
- Preserve the accepted original plate and real source windows. Future authoring must explicitly justify any HDR asset derivative; do not silently retag or rewrite archived artwork, media, previews or original-image attestations.
- First prototype belongs in isolated review mode, with source-native geometry and invisible interaction territories. No live debug chrome or new summary cards.
- Treat this first as steady/localized headroom, not flashing, strobing, forced display-brightness control or autoplaying a full-screen glare. Provide an SDR/Off path without hiding original media or source navigation.
- Keep ordinary SDR output usable and visually complete. CSS white, SDR canvas bloom or a wide-gamut label alone must not be described as true HDR.

## Before any live integration

1. Prove the actual PNG/browser/compositor pipeline and available HDR headroom; do not equate a media query or PQ profile with delivered luminance.
2. Compare a real HDR-capable desktop/phone with SDR, low-power/tone-mapped and unsupported-browser paths. Record device/browser/display settings and limitations without requesting sensor permissions.
3. Check that dynamic headroom changes, resizing and source-window opening do not wash out titles, native media, close controls or the original plate.
4. Keep screenshots/tone-mapped exports explicitly limited: they are not physical HDR brightness proof. Obtain hands-on confirmation before claiming the luminous effect works on Nick's display.
5. Prototype, independently review, and apply normal source/editorial/QA gates before capability release or edition-specific activation. The nomination does not authorize a current-edition retrofit.
